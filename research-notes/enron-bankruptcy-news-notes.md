# Research notes: *Enron Bankruptcy News* (2001–2012)

**What this file is.** A finding aid built from *Enron Bankruptcy News*, a subscription newsletter published by Bankruptcy Creditors' Service, Inc. and edited by Peter A. Chapman (later with co-editors Neil U. Lim and Psyche A. Castillon). It ran 233 issues, from the bankruptcy filing on December 2, 2001 to early 2012.

**What this file is not.** It is not the newsletter and not a source the site may cite for facts. The newsletter itself is copyrighted and is **not** in this repo. Permission from the publisher is pending. Everything below is a pointer, with issue numbers, dates and our own short descriptions, to help find the underlying public documents.

## Rules for agents using these notes

1. **The newsletter is a secondary source.** If it is ever cited, the verb is *reported*, and it must be named: "*Enron Bankruptcy News*, a bankruptcy trade newsletter, reported…". Do not confuse this with a company filing, which on this site also *reports*.
2. **Trace every fact back to the original document** (court order, examiner report, settlement, opinion) and cite that document from `/sources` instead. A fact that exists only in these notes does not go on the site.
3. **Dollar amounts and dates below are as the newsletter reported them.** Verify each against the underlying document before use.
4. **The newsletter can be wrong.** Issue 233 is dated February 15, 2011 in its header, but its contents refer to events of February–March 2012. It is almost certainly a 2012 issue with a typo.

## About the archive

- One plain-text file, about 16 MB and 2.2 million words, containing all 233 issues and about 4,575 numbered articles.
- SHA-256 of the text file: `338e0cc93ffda67e40ec6652738ce2ed347df2500301258088f12ddcbbb04c91`
- Three issues (27, 41 and 74) were distributed free and have a slightly different header ("No Charge"). A search for issue headers will miss them unless it allows for this.
- Coverage is heaviest in 2002–2005 and tapers off as the estate wound down.

**Checks against files we already have:**

- The newsletter describes the Final Report as 140 pages. A contemporary blog post said 137 pages plus about 1,000 pages of appendices. Compare with the page count of `batson-final-report-2003-11-04.pdf` to help confirm the mirror is complete.
- The newsletter's summary of the Second Interim Report says Batson found that six accounting techniques accounted for about 96% of Enron's reported 2000 net income and more than 100% of its reported funds flow. **Striking, but it must be verified in the report itself before use.**

## New material not yet on the site

**A second examiner.** Before Batson, the court appointed Harrison J. Goldin as examiner for Enron North America, the trading subsidiary (issue 19, Mar 19, 2002). He filed roughly 119 weekly and 26 monthly reports, running through 2004. In issue 111 (May 31, 2004), he asked to be relieved of an SPE-related investigation.

**New management would not vouch for the old filings** (issue 40, Aug 21, 2002). Enron's post-bankruptcy CEO and CFO told the SEC they could not certify the 2000 10-K, the 2001 quarterly reports (including Q3), or the November 2001 8-Ks, because current management lacked the knowledge to do so. This is relevant to the Why It Matters page, since personal certification of filings by executives became a Sarbanes-Oxley requirement. Two of the filings they declined to certify are already in `/sources/03-sec-filings`.

**Batson hired accounting professors** (issue 46). Useful for students: academic accountants helped the examiner analyze the transactions.

## The end of the bankruptcy

- Issue 118 (July 17, 2004): court confirms Enron's Fifth Amended Plan.
- Later issues describe Enron as having emerged from bankruptcy in November 2004. From 2007 the newsletter refers to the company as Enron Creditors Recovery Corp.
- Issues 134–233: 29 post-confirmation reports on the wind-down and distributions to creditors.
- Issue 233 (early 2012): the case is reassigned to a new judge as Judge Arthur Gonzalez, who had handled it since 2001, prepares to retire.
