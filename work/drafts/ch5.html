The Collapse

Between mid-October and early December 2001, a quarterly loss, a correction of past results, and a failed merger ended confidence in Enron, and the company filed for bankruptcy.

October 16: the third quarter

On October 16, 2001, Enron announced its results for the third quarter. It reported "recurring" earnings of $0.43 per share. Separately, it reported $1.01 billion of after-tax "non-recurring charges": one-time losses that Enron presented as outside its normal business. They included $287 million for its water business, Azurix, $180 million for restructuring its broadband business, and $544 million for investment losses and for ending early its structured finance arrangements with LJM2. The charges produced a net loss of $618 million for the quarter.source

The Raptor part of those charges was about $710 million before taxes. Sources differ on the after-tax amount. The special committee and the bankruptcy examiner put it at $544 million,sourcesource the figure in Enron's October 16 announcement, where that charge also covered losses on some other investments.source Enron's later quarterly report put the Raptor charges at $462 million after tax.source

On the same day, in a conference call rather than in its written earnings release, Enron disclosed that it would reduce its shareholders' equity by $1.2 billion.source Shareholders' equity is the owners' stake in a company: what it owns minus what it owes. The special committee found that about $1 billion of this cut corrected an accounting error. When Enron issued its own stock to the Raptors in exchange for IOUs (notes receivable), it had counted those IOUs as increases to equity: $172 million in 2000 and $828 million in March 2001. The rest, roughly $200 million, related to ending the Raptors.source Under standard accounting, a company that issues shares in exchange for a promise to pay later generally does not count that promise as new equity until it is paid.

Enron's stock, at $34.30 on October 16 according to the congressional tax staff, fell to $13.90 by the close of trading on October 31.source (A congressman gave a different figure for October 16: $30.72 at the close.source)

Questions multiply

On October 17, the SEC asked Enron to provide information voluntarily about its deals with related parties, and Enron announced the request on October 22. On October 24, Enron announced that Fastow was on leave and would be replaced as chief financial officer.source On October 31, the SEC opened a formal investigation.source In late October, Enron's board also formed a special committee, later chaired by William Powers Jr., to investigate the partnerships.source

The bankruptcy examiner reported that from about October 25 until the bankruptcy filing, a number of senior managers requested and received early payouts of deferred compensation (pay they had earned but chosen to receive later) totaling more than $50 million.source

Enron's credit was now in danger. Enron said that after October 16 its stock price fell and its credit ratings were cut to the lowest level still counted as investment grade. It drew about $3.0 billion from its bank credit lines. Enron itself stated: "Maintaining an investment grade credit rating is a critical element in maintaining liquidity for Enron's wholesale business."source A trader that other companies no longer trust to pay cannot find partners to trade with.

November 8: the restatement

On November 8, 2001, Enron announced that it would restate its financial statements for 1997 through 2000 and the first half of 2001. It said Chewco, JEDI, and an LJM1 entity should have been consolidated all along. It also said that "financial statements for these periods and the audit reports relating to the year-end financial statements for 1997 through 2000 should not be relied upon."source In other words, Enron was telling investors not to rely on four years of its audited results, or on Arthur Andersen's opinions about them.

Enron's quarterly report, filed November 19, gave refined restated figures. Net income fell from $105 million to $26 million for 1997, from $703 million to $564 million for 1998, from $893 million to $635 million for 1999, and from $979 million to $842 million for 2000. Reported debt rose by $711 million, $561 million, $685 million, and $628 million in those years.source Enron's first estimate, on November 8, had been slightly different.source

By November 8 the stock closed at $8.41.source

A rescue attempt

On November 9, Enron and Dynegy announced a proposed merger.source Under the agreement, Enron's shareholders would end up with about 36 percent of the combined company.source On November 13, Dynegy bought $1.5 billion of preferred stock in Enron's Northern Natural Gas pipeline business, and it received an option to buy that pipeline if the merger fell through.source

The quarterly report Enron filed on November 19 showed that the problems were growing. The third-quarter loss, first announced as $618 million, was now reported as $644 million.source Enron disclosed that a credit downgrade on November 12 had made a $690 million debt payable on November 27 unless Enron posted collateral, and that about $3.9 billion more could come due if it lost its investment-grade rating and its stock price stayed low. It warned that an adverse outcome "would likely have a material adverse impact on Enron's ability to continue as a going concern."source A going concern is a business expected to keep operating; doubting it means doubting the company will survive.

That same day, at a meeting with its bankers at the Waldorf Astoria hotel, Enron presented a very different picture of its borrowing. Its balance sheet showed $12.978 billion of debt. But Enron told the banks its debt was $38.094 billion. As Enron itself noted, $25.116 billion was "off-balance sheet," or on the balance sheet but classified as something other than debt.source The examiner's reports put the portion raised through special purpose entities at about $13 billion to $14 billion.sourcesource

The watchdogs

People whose job was to judge Enron from outside were slow to change their views. The Senate Governmental Affairs Committee staff found that all 15 analysts at major Wall Street firms who covered Enron were recommending that investors buy its stock when news of its problems first came out. Three weeks later, after the SEC investigation, Fastow's departure, and the restatement announcement, 10 of the 15 still were.source (Counts differ slightly by date: one senator's statement said 15 of 16 analysts rated Enron "buy" or "strong buy" in early October.source)

November 28 to December 2

On November 28, all three major credit rating agencies cut Enron below investment grade.source The same day, the Dynegy merger unraveled. Enron shut down EnronOnline and suspended most payments, and its stock fell from $4.11 to $0.61. It closed at $0.26 on November 30.source Why Dynegy withdrew is disputed: the bankruptcy examiner wrote that Dynegy abandoned the merger "allegedly because of undisclosed liabilities of Enron,"source and Enron claimed wrongful termination.source

On December 2, 2001, Enron and 13 of its affiliates filed for protection under Chapter 11 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code in New York. The same day, Enron sued Dynegy for at least $10 billion.source Chapter 11 lets a company keep operating while it works out a plan to pay its creditors. According to the congressional tax staff, Enron's was the largest bankruptcy in U.S. history until WorldCom's in July 2002.source

The New York Stock Exchange suspended trading in Enron stock on January 15, 2002. On February 12, 2002, Enron said it did not expect its shareholders to receive anything in the reorganization.source

The tip of the iceberg

The court-appointed bankruptcy examiner wrote that the disclosures about Enron's deals with related parties, such as the partnerships in Chapter 3, may have set off the crisis of confidence. But later information "suggests that the Related-Party Transactions may have been the tip of the iceberg."source In an earlier report, summarized in his final one, he had concluded that in 2000, six accounting techniques produced 96% of Enron's reported net income and allowed it to report $10.2 billion of debt rather than $22.1 billion.source

Banks were involved, too. The Senate subcommittee staff found that in more than $8 billion of transactions known as "prepays," Citigroup and Chase gave Enron "huge loans disguised as energy trades."source Chapter 7 describes what happened to the people and the institutions involved.

Ask Why

Once confidence cracked, Enron went from its October earnings announcement to bankruptcy in about seven weeks, even though the problems behind it had built up over years. Why might a company whose business depends on trust fail so much faster than one whose value lies in things like pipelines and power plants?
