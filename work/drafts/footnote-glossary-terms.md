# Glossary terms needed by "The Footnote" page

Written by the Footnote Annotator. The ids match the `glossary_terms` values in `work/drafts/footnote.json`. The definitions are suggested plain-language wording drawn from general knowledge of accounting concepts; none of them states a fact about Enron. The Powers Report's own glossary (Appendix A of `powers-report-sec`, lines ~7250 onward) defines balance sheet, call, collar, costless collar, consolidated financial statement, credit capacity, derivative, equity method, fair value, fairness opinion and Form 10-K, and the glossary writer may cite it. If another agent's glossary already uses a different id for the same concept, keep theirs and tell the Site Builder which id to map.

| id | term | suggested plain definition | first used |
|---|---|---|---|
| related-party-transaction | related party transaction | A deal between a company and someone closely tied to it, such as an officer, a director, or a business they control. It has to be disclosed because the two sides may not bargain as independent strangers would. | fn-01 |
| form-10-k | Form 10-K | The annual report a U.S. public company must file with the SEC. It includes audited financial statements and their notes. | fn-01 |
| notes-to-financial-statements | notes to the financial statements (footnotes) | Numbered explanations printed after the financial statements that explain the numbers and describe matters the tables cannot show. | fn-01 |
| limited-partnership | limited partnership | A business form, often used for investment funds. A general partner manages it; limited partners contribute money and risk only what they put in. | fn-02 |
| general-partner | general partner / managing member | The party that runs a partnership. The "managing member" runs a company (an LLC) that acts as the general partner. | fn-02 |
| chief-financial-officer | Chief Financial Officer (CFO) | The top executive responsible for a company's finances, including its financial reporting. | fn-03 |
| proxy-statement | proxy statement | A document sent to shareholders before their annual vote. It must describe certain transactions with executives. | fn-03 |
| arms-length | arm's-length transaction | A deal between independent parties, each bargaining for its own interest. | fn-05 |
| auditor | auditor | An independent accounting firm that examines a company's financial statements and gives an opinion on whether they are fairly presented. Enron's auditor was Arthur Andersen. | fn-05 |
| audit-committee | audit committee | A committee of the board of directors that oversees financial reporting and the auditor. Enron's was called the Audit and Compliance Committee. | fn-05 |
| hedge | hedge | A contract meant to offset a possible loss: if an investment falls, the hedge gains. A real hedge moves risk to an outside party able to pay. | fn-06 |
| merchant-investment | merchant investments | Enron's term for stakes in other companies that it held to sell later, valued at market price each quarter. | fn-06 |
| mark-to-market | mark-to-market accounting | Valuing an asset at its current market price each reporting period and counting the change as profit or loss. | fn-06 |
| special-purpose-entity | special purpose entity (SPE) | A separate company set up for one narrow purpose. Under the rules of the time, a company could leave an SPE off its own books if an independent owner put in at least 3% of its capital at risk and controlled it. | fn-07 |
| balance-sheet | balance sheet | A snapshot of what a company owns (assets), owes (liabilities), and the owners' stake (equity) on a given date. | fn-07 |
| consolidation | consolidation | Combining a subsidiary's (or SPE's) assets, debts and results into the parent company's own financial statements. | fn-07 |
| notes-payable | notes payable | Written IOUs a company owes to others. | fn-08 |
| notes-receivable | notes receivable | Written IOUs that others owe to the company. | fn-10 |
| restricted-stock | restricted shares | Shares that cannot be sold (or hedged) for a set period, and are therefore usually valued at a discount. | fn-08 |
| common-stock | common stock | Ordinary ownership shares in a company. | fn-08 |
| warrant | warrant | A right to buy a company's shares at a set price, usually for years; economically similar to an option. | fn-09 |
| equity-method | equity method investee | A company in which the investor owns a large minority stake (often 20-50%) and reports its share of that company's profits, without combining the two companies' books. | fn-09 |
| unconsolidated-affiliate | unconsolidated (equity) affiliate | A company the investor partly owns but does not combine into its own financial statements. | fn-30 |
| derivative | derivative | A contract whose value depends on something else, such as a stock price or an interest rate. | fn-09 |
| basis | carryover basis / basis | The amount at which an asset is carried on the company's books. A "basis of zero" means the asset was on the books at nothing. | fn-10 |
| fairness-opinion | fairness opinion | A professional firm's written opinion on whether the price in a deal is fair. | fn-10 |
| initial-public-offering | initial public offering (IPO) | The first sale of a company's shares to the public. | fn-10 |
| shareholders-equity | shareholders' equity | The owners' stake on the balance sheet: assets minus liabilities. | fn-11 |
| demand-note | demand note | An IOU that must be repaid whenever the lender asks. | fn-12 |
| option | option | A contract giving the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell something at a set price. | fn-13 |
| put-option | put option | An option to sell at a set price. It gains value when the price falls. | fn-13 |
| share-settled | share-settled | Settled by delivering shares, not cash. | fn-13 |
| strike-price | strike price | The set price at which an option can be used. | fn-25 |
| in-the-money | in the money | An option that would already be worth something if used today. | fn-25 |
| collar | collar | A pair of options that sets a floor and a ceiling on a stock price. Below the floor one side pays; above the ceiling the other side pays. | fn-15 |
| equity-transaction | equity transaction | A transaction in a company's own shares. It changes shareholders' equity but is not counted as profit or loss. | fn-15 |
| credit-capacity | credit capacity | The ability of a party to pay what it owes. | fn-15 |
| notional-amount | notional amount | The face size of the assets a derivative refers to. It is used to calculate payments and is not itself money that changes hands. | fn-16 |
| revenue | revenue | Money a company records as earned from its business, before expenses. | fn-17 |
| pre-tax-earnings | pre-tax earnings (income) | Profit before income taxes are subtracted. | fn-17 |
| net-income | net income | The bottom-line profit after all expenses and taxes. | fn-17 |
| reserve | reserve (credit reserve) | An amount charged against profit to reflect money the company may not collect. | fn-18 |
| price-risk-management | price risk management activities | Enron's term for its trading and hedging of energy and other prices. | fn-18 |
| interest-income | interest income / interest expense | Interest earned on money lent out / interest paid on money borrowed. | fn-19 |
| lock-up | lock-up agreement | A promise, usually made around an IPO, not to sell shares for a set period. | fn-20 |
| forward-contract | forward contract | An agreement to buy or sell something at a fixed price on a future date. | fn-21 |
| gaap | generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) | The rules U.S. companies must follow in preparing financial statements. | fn-21 |
| stock-split | stock split | Dividing each existing share into more shares (for example, one into two). Share counts change but ownership does not. | fn-22 |
| restatement | restatement | Re-issuing past financial statements to correct errors. | fn-23 |
| pecuniary-interest | pecuniary interest | A financial stake: the chance to gain or lose money. | fn-24 |
| code-of-conduct | code of conduct | A company's written ethics rules for employees. Enron's barred officers from profiting from entities that did business with Enron unless the Chairman and CEO approved (source for the Enron part: `rpt-psi-board`, printed p. 24, footnote 58, PDF p. 28). | fn-24 |
| unwind | unwind | To end a set of linked financial contracts before they run their course. | fn-26 |
| dark-fiber | dark fiber | Fiber-optic cable that has been laid but is not yet in use ("lit"). | fn-27 |
| quarter-end | quarter-end | The last day of a three-month reporting period (for Enron: March 31, June 30, September 30, December 31). | fn-27 |
| gross-margin | gross margin | Sales price minus the cost of what was sold. | fn-28 |
| securitization | securitization | Pooling assets and selling investors securities backed by the pool. | fn-29 |
| counterparty | counterparty | The other party to a contract or deal. | fn-29 |
| trust | trust | A legal arrangement in which one party holds assets for the benefit of others. SPEs were often set up as trusts. | fn-30 |
| side-deal | side deal | An undocumented or undisclosed promise made alongside a formal contract. | fn-31 |
