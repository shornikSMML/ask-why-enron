# Glossary terms used in The Story (chapters 1-7)

Written by the Story Writer for the Reference Writer. Each id is the `data-term` value used in `work/drafts/ch1.html` ... `ch7.html`. Where the Footnote Annotator already proposed the same id (`work/drafts/footnote-glossary-terms.md`), I used that id, marked "(shared)". The definitions are plain-language suggestions from general knowledge. None of them states a fact about Enron. Facts about Enron in the chapters are cited to fact cards.

| id | term as shown | suggested one-line definition | first used in |
|---|---|---|---|
| assets | assets | Everything a company owns or is owed that has value: cash, buildings, pipelines, contracts, investments. | ch1 |
| revenue | revenue (shared) | Money a company records as earned from its business, before any costs are subtracted. Not the same as profit. | ch1, ch2 |
| auditor | auditor (shared) | An independent accounting firm that checks a company's financial statements and says whether they present its finances fairly. | ch1, ch6 |
| forward-contract | forward contract (shared) | An agreement to buy or sell something at a fixed price on a future date. | ch1 |
| mark-to-market | mark-to-market accounting (shared) | Valuing a contract or investment at its current market value each period and counting any change as profit or loss right away. | ch1, ch2 |
| net-income | net income (shared) | The bottom-line profit after all costs and taxes. | ch1, ch2 |
| market-capitalization | market capitalization | The total stock-market value of a company: its share price times the number of shares. | ch1 |
| fair-value | fair value | The price an asset would sell for in an orderly sale today; when no market price exists, it has to be estimated. | ch2 |
| unrealized-gain | unrealized gain | An increase in the value of something a company still holds, counted before any cash is received. | ch2 |
| sec | Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) | The federal agency that regulates the U.S. securities markets and the financial reports public companies must file. | ch2, ch4, ch5 |
| merchant-investment | merchant investments (shared) | Enron's term for stakes in other companies that it held to sell later and valued at market price each quarter. | ch2, ch3 |
| credit-rating | credit rating | A grade from an agency such as Standard & Poor's or Moody's on how likely a borrower is to repay its debts. | ch2 |
| investment-grade | investment grade | The higher credit ratings, signaling a relatively safe borrower; ratings below this level are often called "junk." | ch2, ch5 |
| balance-sheet | balance sheet (shared) | A snapshot of what a company owns (assets), owes (liabilities), and the owners' stake (equity) on a given date. | ch2, ch3 |
| off-balance-sheet | off-balance-sheet | Describes assets, debts, or deals that are kept off a company's own balance sheet, often by placing them in a separate entity. | ch2 |
| special-purpose-entity | special purpose entity (SPE) (shared) | A separate company or partnership set up for one narrow purpose. Under the rules of the time, a company could leave an SPE off its books if an independent owner put in at least 3% of its capital at risk and controlled it. | ch3 |
| consolidation | consolidate / consolidation (shared) | Combining a subsidiary's or SPE's assets, debts, and results into the parent company's own financial statements. | ch3, ch5 |
| proxy-statement | proxy statement (shared) | A document sent to shareholders before their annual vote; it must describe certain dealings with executives. | ch3 |
| chief-financial-officer | chief financial officer (CFO) (shared) | The top executive in charge of a company's finances and financial reporting. | ch3 |
| general-partner | general partner (shared) | The party that runs a partnership and makes its decisions; the investors ("limited partners") mainly supply money. | ch3 |
| conflict-of-interest | conflict of interest | A situation in which a person has duties to one party but a personal stake that could pull against those duties. | ch3 |
| hedge | hedge (shared) | A contract meant to offset a possible loss: if an investment falls, the hedge pays. A real hedge moves the risk to an outside party able to pay. | ch3 |
| put-option | put option (shared) | The right, but not the obligation, to sell something at a set price. It gains value when the price falls. | ch3 |
| audit-committee | Audit Committee (shared) | A committee of the board of directors that oversees financial reporting and the outside auditor. Enron's was called the Audit and Compliance Committee. | ch4 |
| cpa | certified public accountant (CPA) | An accountant licensed by a state after passing the national CPA exam and meeting experience requirements. | ch4 |
| related-party-transaction | related-party transaction / deals with related parties (shared) | A deal between a company and someone closely tied to it, such as an officer or an entity an officer controls. | ch4 |
| shareholders-equity | shareholders' equity (shared) | The owners' stake on the balance sheet: assets minus liabilities. | ch5 |
| notes-receivable | notes receivable (shared) | Written IOUs that others owe to the company. | ch5 |
| deferred-compensation | deferred compensation | Pay an employee has earned but has chosen, or agreed, to receive at a later date. | ch5 |
| restatement | restatement / restate (shared) | Re-issuing past financial statements to correct errors. | ch5 |
| going-concern | going concern | A business expected to keep operating for the foreseeable future. A "going concern" warning means there is real doubt that it will. | ch5 |
| chapter-11 | Chapter 11 bankruptcy | A form of U.S. bankruptcy that lets a company keep operating while it works out a court-approved plan to pay its creditors. | ch5 |
| auditor-independence | auditor independence | The principle that an auditor must be free of financial or personal ties that could bias its judgment about a client's books. | ch6 |
| non-audit-services | non-audit services | Work an audit firm sells to an audit client beyond the audit itself, such as consulting, tax, or systems work. | ch6 |
| fact-finder | fact-finder | The judge or jury that decides what the facts are in a trial. The bankruptcy examiner said what a fact-finder *could* conclude, not what one did. | ch6, ch7 |
| gaap | generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) (shared) | The rules U.S. companies must follow in preparing financial statements. | ch6 |
| fiduciary-duty | fiduciary duty | A legal duty to act in another's best interest; officers and directors owe fiduciary duties to their company and its shareholders. | ch7 |
| 401k | 401(k) plan | A U.S. workplace retirement savings plan in which employees invest part of their pay, sometimes with a matching contribution from the employer. | ch7 |
| sarbanes-oxley | Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) | The 2002 federal law that tightened rules for public companies' financial reporting and created the PCAOB. (Date and provisions: cite cards C-070 to C-080.) | ch7 |
| pcaob | Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) | The nonprofit board created by Sarbanes-Oxley to register, set standards for, and inspect the firms that audit public companies. (Cite card C-072.) | ch7 |
| internal-control | internal control (over financial reporting) | The procedures a company uses to make sure its financial records are accurate and its assets are protected. | ch7 |
