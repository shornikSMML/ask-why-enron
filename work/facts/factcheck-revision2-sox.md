## Ruling 1: does the SEC rule count nine banned categories? (T-034, S-042)

**It does not settle the question, and the earlier ruling stands.** Three different SEC-related counts exist:

1. **The statute** (Sec. 201, 10A(g)) numbers nine items. The ninth is "any other service that the Board determines, by regulation, is impermissible."
2. **The SEC's January 2003 rule release** (33-8183, line 154) describes the *Act* as setting out "nine categories", then lists the same nine, with the catch-all last. The **SEC's Section 704 report** says "eight categories", counting only the named services.
3. **The SEC's rule text itself** (Rule 2-01(c)(4)(i)-(x), lines 1107-1143) lists **ten** prohibited services. It splits management functions from human resources, and legal from expert services, and has no catch-all.

T-034's claim is accurate: the release describes the Act as listing nine. Its note ("the SEC's own rule counts nine") was wrong, and I corrected it.

**Site wording is unchanged:** "The Act bans eight named kinds of non-audit services, plus any other service the PCAOB bans by rule." The site may add "the SEC's 2003 rule release counts these as nine categories". Never write "the SEC rule counts nine".

## Ruling 2: SSAE No. 10 as the interim standard (T-011)

**It is supported, but only as the SEC worded it.** Rel. 33-8238 (line 358) says:
- On April 16, 2003 the PCAOB designated "Statements on Standards for Attestation Engagements as existed on April 16" as the standard for attestations on internal control, pending further PCAOB standard-setting.
- On April 25 the SEC approved that action.
- "SSAE No. 10 is thus the standard applicable on a transition basis."

Limits:
- The source does not say "interim".
- It does not say which April 25 order carried the approval.
- It says nothing about the interim *auditing* standards.

So S-019 and Reader D's gap 1 stand: the site still cannot say what the April 25 orders (Rel. 33-8222/8223) contained.

What came next is in the library:
- **AS No. 2:** final in March 2004, SEC-approved June 17, 2004. It "governs both the auditor's assessment of controls and its attestation" (GAO-06-361 p. 20).
- **AS No. 5:** replaced AS No. 2 on July 25, 2007 (GAO-08-163 p. 14).

I removed T-011's note calling AS No. 2 "not in the library".

## Other findings writers must know

- **Sec. 404 start date (T-008): correction to the gap map.** Revision-2 gap-map suggestion 1 says compliance "began with fiscal years ending on or after June 15, 2004." That is wrong. June 15, 2004 was the date in the June 2003 rule. The SEC extended it on February 24, 2004 to November 15, 2004 for accelerated filers and July 15, 2005 for other companies (GAO-06-361 p. 27). GAO-08-163 says accelerated filers were "first required to comply ... for fiscal years ending on or after November 15, 2004." Smaller companies got further extensions; GAO-08-163 gives years ending after December 15, 2007 for 404(a) and December 15, 2008 for 404(b), and the 2010 exemption (T-026) then removed 404(b) for them.
- **Reader D's gaps partly filled by the GAO reports:**
  - Gap 2: GAO-08-163 p. 14 says the PCAOB issued its first audit standard on December 17, 2003, and the SEC approved it on May 14, 2004. The standard is not named, and its text is not in the library.
  - AS No. 2's dates are in GAO-06-361 p. 20.
- **Free Enterprise Fund:**
  - For the holdings, cite the opinion: Part III (p. 492); pp. 508-509 (removal restrictions invalid, Board removable at will, Board's existence valid); p. 510 (Appointments Clause challenges have no merit).
  - "The Board may continue to function as before" is the Syllabus's wording. Label it as the Syllabus.
- **The $1.24 billion (T-012)** is the SEC's estimate from before anyone had complied, and it leaves out the auditor's attestation. Never present it as the actual cost.
- **S-042 and S-045:** I added cross-references to T-034 and T-002 to their checker notes. The claims did not change.
